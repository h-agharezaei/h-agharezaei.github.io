Big-O describes growth, not speed: O(n) means the cost grows at most in proportion to n for large n. Constants and smaller terms are dropped, so two O(n) algorithms can differ a lot in real time. See [Binary search](/glossary/binary-search/) (O(log n)) and [Amortized analysis](/glossary/amortized-analysis/).
